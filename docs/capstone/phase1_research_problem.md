## 1. System Framing & Operational Boundary

MedMatch is formally defined as:

> **An evidence-grounded retrieval-augmented clinical trial eligibility matching system that determines whether a patient appears eligible for clinical trials based on available clinical information and provides supporting evidence for its decisions.**

### 1.1 Clinical Decision-Support Framing
MedMatch is strictly a **clinical decision-support research prototype** designed to assist clinical research coordinators, trial investigators, and oncologists. 

- **Non-Autonomous Principle:** MedMatch **does not make autonomous clinical decisions**, does not prescribe interventions, and does not enroll patients in clinical trials.
- **Human-in-the-Loop Requirement:** All trial eligibility determinations, criterion evaluations, and evidence citations generated by MedMatch serve as structured recommendations subject to mandatory human clinical review.
- **Decision Scope:** The system evaluates *apparent eligibility* based strictly on recorded medical evidence present in the patient record at a given point in time. It explicitly distinguishes between known compliance, known disqualification, and missing/unverified clinical data.

---

## 2. Background and Motivation

Clinical trials are the scientific foundation of evidence-based oncology and therapeutics, yet patient recruitment remains one of the largest bottlenecks in translational medicine. Up to **80% of clinical trials fail to meet recruitment deadlines**, and roughly **50% of trial sites recruit one or zero patients**. 

Matching cancer patients to appropriate clinical trials is cognitively demanding and labor-intensive due to:
1. **Proliferation of Eligibility Criteria:** Contemporary Phase II/III oncology protocols frequently list 20 to 50 complex inclusion and exclusion criteria encompassing histological subtypes, genomic alterations, prior lines of systemic therapy, organ-specific laboratory thresholds, and performance scores.
2. **Unstructured Patient Records:** Longitudinal Electronic Health Record (EHR) data resides primarily in freeform clinical progress notes, pathology summaries, and radiology reports, presenting challenges in negation detection, temporal reasoning, and biomarker extraction.
3. **Registry Scale and Latency:** ClinicalTrials.gov and institutional trial registries index hundreds of thousands of active protocols, making manual screening across multiple registries intractable for clinical staff.
4. **The "Black-Box" LLM Trap:** While modern Large Language Models (LLMs) possess vast biomedical knowledge, naive zero-shot querying of LLMs over clinical notes leads to unsupported inferences, medical hallucinations, uncalibrated confidence, and lack of verifiable provenance back to the source text.

## 3. The Core Research Problem

### 3.1 Problem Statement
Given an unstructured clinical note $P$ representing a patient encounter and a repository of clinical trial protocols $\mathcal{T} = \{T_1, T_2, \dots, T_N\}$, the objective is to:
1. Efficiently and accurately retrieve a candidate subset of relevant trials $\mathcal{T}_{cand} \subset \mathcal{T}$ and their individual eligibility criteria $C$;
2. Evaluate each criterion $c \in C$ against patient evidence $E_P$ extracted from $P$;
3. Produce a calibrated, evidence-grounded trial-level eligibility classification $Y \in \{\text{ELIGIBLE}, \text{INELIGIBLE}, \text{NEEDS\_REVIEW}\}$;
4. Provide verifiable bidirectional provenance linking every criterion determination directly to explicit spans of patient text and protocol text.

### 3.2 Current System Limitations (Phase 0 Audit Findings)
As verified in the Phase 0 audit (`docs/capstone/phase0_system_audit.md` and `docs/capstone/ai_pipeline_baseline.md`), the current MedMatch baseline features:
- **Monolithic Freeform Retrieval:** Searches whole trials using a 384-dimensional dense sentence embedding (`sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) via pgvector cosine distance, retrieving unindexed top-$k=5$ candidate trials based on raw note text.
- **Biomarker and Lexical Blindness:** Dense embeddings struggle with exact lexical matches (e.g., specific gene mutations like *EGFR exon 20 insertion*, exact lab cutoffs such as *platelets $\ge 100 \times 10^9/\text{L}$*, and TNM staging *T2N0M0*).
- **Single-Prompt Black-Box Reasoning:** Feeds the entire raw note and top-$k$ retrieved trial criteria blocks into Google Gemini 2.5 Flash in a single 966-line prompt (`trial_matching_prompt.py`), relying entirely on internal LLM attention without intermediate criterion-level validation.
- **No Persistence or Provenance:** Evaluated matches are returned in-memory and discarded without persisting criterion-level evidence spans to the database (`MatchRepository` is never written by `MatchingService`).
- **Complete Lack of Evaluation Ground Truth:** The system has no benchmark dataset, no gold-standard labels, no deterministic train/val/test splits, and no automated metrics for retrieval recall, classification F1, or grounding fidelity.

## 4. The Research Gap

Existing literature and current tools fall into one of two extremes:
1. **Rule-Based & Information Extraction Systems:** Rely on rigid clinical ontologies, regex, and formal query languages (e.g., SQL/SPARQL over CT.gov XML). These systems suffer from high brittleness, failing when freeform clinical phrasing deviates from anticipated patterns.
2. **Pure Generative LLM Matchers:** Pass patient notes and trial text directly to high-capacity commercial LLMs. While linguistically flexible, they suffer from hallucinated biomarker statuses, incorrect numerical boundary evaluations, opaque reasoning chains, and unacceptable operational cost at scale.

**The Research Gap:**  
There is an absence of an **empirically validated, modular, evidence-grounded retrieval-augmented framework** that:
- Deconstructs trial protocols into atomic, typed criteria;
- Combines sparse lexical precision with dense semantic recall;
- Enforces strict criterion-level tri-state reasoning (`PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNKNOWN`) grounded in explicit textual spans;
- Applies deterministic clinical aggregation rules;
- Systematically surfaces clinical uncertainty (`NEEDS_REVIEW`) rather than forcing speculative classifications.

## 5. Scope and Boundaries

### 5.1 In Scope (Phase 1 Specification & Future Implementation)
- Definition of formal task representations and aggregation rules.
- Ground-truth schema for both criterion-level and trial-level annotations.
- Comprehensive metrics across retrieval, classification, grounding, calibration, and error taxonomy.
- Benchmark dataset strategy combining curated public trial cohorts (TREC, TrialGPT) and rule-derived synthetic profiles.
- Reproducibility and experiment tracking specifications.

### 5.2 Out of Scope (Explicit Non-Goals)
- Autonomous clinical trial enrollment or physician replacement.
- Real-time continuous EHR streaming integration (FHIR server subscription listeners).
- Image-based clinical trial matching (e.g., evaluating DICOM CT/MRI scans directly).
- Real-world drug supply chain, insurance pre-authorization, or clinical trial site budgeting.

## 6. Structural Success Criteria

In accordance with rigorous capstone standards, project success is defined **structurally and methodologically** rather than by arbitrary target numbers (e.g., asserting "F1 > 95%" without prior measurement):

1. **Deterministic Reproducibility:** Every experiment from E0 to E4 can be re-run end-to-end using frozen configuration files, locked dependencies, and fixed random seeds.
2. **Documented Ground Truth:** Benchmark annotations adhere to a validated schema with explicit criterion-level and trial-level labels and character-level patient evidence spans.
3. **Strictly Held-Out Test Set:** Zero data leakage across patient and trial splits; the test split remains completely untouched until final benchmark evaluation.
4. **Reproducible Baselines:** The baseline performance of both the LLM-only pipeline (E0) and the current MedMatch system (E1) is quantified and published as the empirical reference floor.
5. **Automated Evaluation Harness:** Metrics across retrieval (Recall@K, MRR), classification (Macro-F1, Confusion Matrix), grounding (Unsupported Claim Rate, Grounded Decision Rate), and calibration (ECE) are computed automatically without manual spreadsheet compilation.
6. **Systematic Error Categorization:** 100% of prediction errors on the test set are classifiable into the 12-factor error taxonomy (E1–E12) to guide iterative improvement.
7. **Verifiable Bidirectional Provenance:** Every non-unknown criterion decision is mapped to verifiable verbatim text spans from the patient note and trial protocol.
8. **Statistically Validated Comparisons:** All architectural comparisons are validated using appropriate paired statistical hypothesis tests (McNemar's test, Wilcoxon signed-rank, bootstrap confidence intervals).

### 7.1 Defensible Capstone Framing
The core contribution of this capstone is:
> **A modular, evidence-grounded retrieval-augmented framework for clinical trial eligibility matching that integrates hybrid semantic-lexical retrieval, structured clinical entity representation, decomposed criterion-level reasoning, explicit textual provenance attribution, and uncertainty-aware decision calibration.**
